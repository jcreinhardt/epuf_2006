\section{How much of the data is censored}

Every cross-section fit is doubly Type-I censored: earnings at or above
$\mathrm{HIGHC} = \mathrm{taxmax}(\text{year}) - \$1000$ enter the likelihood only as
``somewhere above the cap''. Figure~\ref{fig:censshare} shows how much of each cell that
is --- the share $n_{\text{high}}/n$ of a (year, sex, single-year-age) cell at the
taxable maximum --- on a birth-cohort~$\times$~age grid, so a calendar year runs along an
anti-diagonal and a single cohort's life-cycle path reads straight up a column. Both
panels share one color scale, so men and women are directly comparable. Cells are the
estimation sample (those with at least 1000 positive-earnings observations); cells
outside it are blank.

\begin{figure}[h!]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{presentation/08_24/figures/censored_share_cohort_age.pdf}
\caption{Share of workers with earnings at the taxable maximum, i.e.\ censored for the
purposes of estimation, by birth cohort and age. Empirical shares from EPUF, not the
fitted $P(\text{at cap})$.}
\label{fig:censshare}
\end{figure}

The bright band is the tight-cap era of the 1950s and 1960s: at its peak roughly
$78\%$ of prime-age men in a cell are at the cap, and the average across men's cells over
the whole sample is $28\%$ (women: $7.9\%$, peaking at $34\%$). Where the band is bright
the cell's own observations say almost nothing about the upper tail --- the interior
holds only the quarter of the distribution below the cap --- which is why the tail there
is pinned by the aggregate constraint rather than identified locally. The band fades
after the 1972 amendments indexed the taxable maximum to wages: from 1980 on the censored
share averages $10.9\%$ across men's cells (at most $32\%$, in the peak-earning ages) and
$2.0\%$ across women's, and the fits are correspondingly informative about the tail.

\section{Where the fitted distributions miss GKOS (2022)}

The same cohort~$\times$~age layout answers a second question: where in the plane do the
fitted parametric distributions disagree with published moments and quantiles? The
benchmark is the GKSW/GKOS cohort~$\times$~age file (Guvenen, Kaplan, Ocampo and Song,
\emph{Lifetime Earnings in the United States over Six Decades}, 2022), \texttt{sel0}
selection, ages 25--55 and years 1957--2013, in real 2013 dollars. Every model
functional is computed from the fitted law itself --- log-moments by quadrature on the
log-density, quantiles by root-finding on the CDF --- conditional on
$X \ge Y_{\min}(t) = 260 \times$ nominal minimum wage, which is the screen \texttt{sel0}
already carries, and deflated with GKSW's own PCE matrix. This is the per-cell version of
the averaged line plots in \texttt{plot\_guv\_comparison.py}.

Each functional gets its own figure, men and women side by side on one shared symmetric
color scale with limits at the 98th percentile of $|\text{gap}|$. Gaps are signed
\emph{model minus data}, so the colormap is diverging and centered on zero. Cells to the
right of the dashed anti-diagonal are calendar years after 2006 and use extrapolated
rather than fitted parameters.

\subsection*{Two unit systems}

Every functional is plotted twice, and the pair is what separates two different questions.

\emph{Absolute} (Figures~\ref{fig:gap-meanlog}--\ref{fig:gap-p98}) puts the gap in the
functional's own units: real 2013 dollars for the quantiles, log units for the mean and
standard deviation of logs, dimensionless for skewness and kurtosis. It says how many
dollars the fit is off by. Its weakness is that real earnings rise across the window, so
a constant \emph{proportional} error grows in dollars and the color limits end up set by
the later, richer cells; early cohorts look quiet even where the relative miss is large.
The published $p98$, for instance, has a median of \$112{,}000 across men's cells in
1957--72 and \$212{,}000 in 1980--2006, so the same dollar gap means very different
things at the two ends.

\emph{Log points} (Figures~\ref{fig:gap-meanlog-lp}--\ref{fig:gap-p98-lp}) plots
$100 \times [\log \text{model} - \log \text{data}]$ instead, which puts every year on the
same footing and makes the early cohorts readable. It covers the level functionals only
--- mean log earnings and the six quantiles. The standard deviation, skewness and
kurtosis of logs are already scale-free, so their absolute figure \emph{is} their
relative figure and no duplicate is written; skewness also changes sign in these data,
which rules out a ratio outright.

One difference between the two is worth anticipating. Men's dollar errors are an order of
magnitude larger than women's, so on the absolute upper-tail figures the shared
within-figure scale leaves the women's panel comparatively flat. In log points the two
sexes are of comparable magnitude and the women's structure becomes visible --- both are
true statements about the same fit.

\subsection*{The concept wedge}

One caveat governs the level panels specifically. GKSW measure W-2 wage and salary income
of commerce-and-industry workers in the CWHS; EPUF measures covered earnings of all
covered workers. That concept and coverage wedge is worth roughly $7$--$10\%$ in the early
decades and shrinks as coverage expands, so a systematic negative offset in mean log
earnings and in the middle quantiles is expected and is not model misfit. The shape
functionals and the \emph{curvature} of the gaps across the plane are the parts that
speak to fit.

\subsection*{What the panels show}

The middle of the distribution is where the fits do best, and where the two sexes differ
most. Women's median is within \$2{,}000 of the published value in $97\%$ of cells and
their gap in mean log earnings has a median of $+1.5$ log points over the whole plane ---
essentially the concept wedge and nothing more. Men's median is within \$2{,}000 in only
$37\%$ of cells, and their mean log earnings sit about $-9.6$ log points low throughout,
which is the larger wedge one expects where covered-versus-W2 coverage differences bite
hardest.

The tails are where the plane stops being flat, and the pattern lines up with the
censoring map of Figure~\ref{fig:censshare}. Men's $p98$ is the worst panel by a wide
margin: a median gap of $-\$25{,}400$, or $-27$ log points, over the tight-cap years
1957--72 --- exactly the cohorts and ages where three quarters of the cell is at the
taxable maximum and the upper tail is pinned by the aggregate constraint rather than
identified from the cell's own observations --- against $-\$16{,}700$, or $-8$ log
points, over 1980--2006. This is the case where the two unit systems disagree in
emphasis: in dollars the gap narrows only modestly, while in log points it shrinks by
more than a factor of three, and the log-point reading is the one that tracks the
censored share. Women run the other way, $+\$17{,}600$ ($+36$ log points) at $p98$ in the
tight-cap years against $+\$9{,}600$ ($+11$ log points) after 1980, the second mixture
component overshooting off a thin upper tail. In both cases the relative tail error falls
as the censored share falls, which is the expected signature of a censoring-driven
identification problem rather than a wrong parametric family.

Among the shape functionals, men's kurtosis of logs sits systematically below the data
(median $-0.99$ over the whole plane, $-1.37$ in the tight-cap years), so the fitted
Normal--Laplace log-density is not heavy-tailed enough where the data are most censored;
the standard deviation of logs is close throughout (median $+0.026$ for men, $+0.041$ for
women). The post-2006 extrapolated block behaves better on the tails than the tight-cap
block does, which is a statement about the 2000--04 shapes it freezes, not independent
evidence that the extrapolation is right; note also that it flips the sign of women's
upper-tail gap, from $+\$9{,}600$ at $p98$ in 1980--2006 to $-\$23{,}300$ after 2006.

\subsection*{Reproducing these figures}

The plotting code lives in this folder alongside the report. Both scripts are run from
the project root:

\begin{verbatim}
python presentation/08_24/plot_censored_share.py
python presentation/08_24/plot_guv_gap_heatmaps.py
\end{verbatim}

\noindent The second writes both unit systems by default;
\texttt{-{}-units absolute|logpoints} restricts it, \texttt{-{}-params} swaps the
parameter surface, and \texttt{-{}-reuse} replots from the cached per-cell values in
\texttt{output/cross\_sections/guv\_gap\_cells.csv} instead of recomputing the
quadrature and root-finding. Figures land in \texttt{presentation/08\_24/figures/}.
