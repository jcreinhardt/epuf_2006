\section{How much of the data is censored}

Every cross-section fit is doubly Type-I censored: earnings at or above
$\mathrm{HIGHC} = \mathrm{taxmax}(\text{year}) - \$1000$ enter the likelihood only as
``somewhere above the cap''. Figure~\ref{fig:censshare} shows how much of each cell that
is --- the share $n_{\text{high}}/n$ of a (year, sex, single-year-age) cell at the
taxable maximum --- on a birth-cohort~$\times$~age grid, so a calendar year runs along an
anti-diagonal and a single cohort's life-cycle path reads straight up a column. Both
panels share one color scale, so men and women are directly comparable. Cells are the
estimation sample (those with at least 1000 positive-earnings observations); cells
outside it are blank.

\begin{figure}[h!]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{output/cross_sections/plots/censored_share_cohort_age.pdf}
\caption{Share of workers with earnings at the taxable maximum, i.e.\ censored for the
purposes of estimation, by birth cohort and age. Empirical shares from EPUF, not the
fitted $P(\text{at cap})$.}
\label{fig:censshare}
\end{figure}

The bright band is the tight-cap era of the 1950s and 1960s: at its peak roughly
$78\%$ of prime-age men in a cell are at the cap, and the average across men's cells over
the whole sample is $28\%$ (women: $7.9\%$, peaking at $34\%$). Where the band is bright
the cell's own observations say almost nothing about the upper tail --- the interior
holds only the quarter of the distribution below the cap --- which is why the tail there
is pinned by the aggregate constraint rather than identified locally. The band fades
after the 1972 amendments indexed the taxable maximum to wages: from 1980 on the censored
share averages $10.9\%$ across men's cells (at most $32\%$, in the peak-earning ages) and
$2.0\%$ across women's, and the fits are correspondingly informative about the tail.

\section{Where the fitted distributions miss GKOS (2022)}

The same cohort~$\times$~age layout answers a second question: where in the plane do the
fitted parametric distributions disagree with published moments and quantiles? The
benchmark is the GKSW/GKOS cohort~$\times$~age file (Guvenen, Kaplan, Ocampo and Song,
\emph{Lifetime Earnings in the United States over Six Decades}, 2022), \texttt{sel0}
selection, ages 25--55 and years 1957--2013, in real 2013 dollars. Every model
functional is computed from the fitted law itself --- log-moments by quadrature on the
log-density, quantiles by root-finding on the CDF --- conditional on
$X \ge Y_{\min}(t) = 260 \times$ nominal minimum wage, which is the screen \texttt{sel0}
already carries, and deflated with GKSW's own PCE matrix. This is the per-cell version of
the averaged line plots in \texttt{plot\_guv\_comparison.py}.

Gaps are signed \emph{model minus data}, so the color scale is diverging and centered on
zero. Mean log earnings and the dollar quantiles are reported in log points
($100 \times [\log \text{model} - \log \text{data}]$), because dollar quantiles span an
order of magnitude over the sample and a raw dollar gap would mostly re-plot the price
level; $\mathrm{sd}$, skewness and kurtosis of logs are already scale-free and are
differenced directly. Cells to the right of the dashed anti-diagonal are calendar years
after 2006 and use extrapolated rather than fitted parameters.

One caveat governs how to read the level panels. GKSW measure W-2 wage and salary income
of commerce-and-industry workers in the CWHS; EPUF measures covered earnings of all
covered workers. That concept and coverage wedge is worth roughly $7$--$10\%$ in the early
decades and shrinks as coverage expands, so a systematic negative offset in mean log
earnings and in the middle quantiles is expected and is not model misfit. The parts that
speak to fit are the shape functionals and the \emph{curvature} of the quantile gaps
across the plane.

\subsection*{What the panels show}

The middle of the distribution is where the fits do best. Men's $p75$ is within $5$ log
points of the published value in $66\%$ of cells and $p50$ in $35\%$; for women the same
figures are $91\%$ and $81\%$. Women's median gap in mean log earnings is $+1.5$ log
points over the whole plane, i.e.\ essentially the concept wedge and nothing more.

The tails are where the plane stops being flat, and the pattern lines up with the
censoring map of Figure~\ref{fig:censshare}. Men's $p98$ is the worst panel by a wide
margin: its median gap is $-27$ log points over the tight-cap years 1957--72 --- exactly
the cohorts and ages where three quarters of the cell is at the taxable maximum and the
upper tail is pinned by the aggregate constraint rather than identified from the cell's
own observations --- against $-8$ log points over 1980--2006, once the indexed cap leaves
the top of the distribution visible. Women run the other way, $+36$ log points at $p98$
in the tight-cap years and $+11$ after 1980: the second mixture component is fitted off a
thin upper tail and overshoots. In both cases the tail error shrinks by roughly a factor
of three as the censored share falls, which is the expected signature of a
censoring-driven identification problem rather than a wrong parametric family.

Among the shape functionals, men's kurtosis of logs sits systematically below the data
(median $-0.99$ over the whole plane, $-1.37$ in the tight-cap years), so the fitted
Normal--Laplace log-density is not heavy-tailed enough where the data are most censored;
$\mathrm{sd}$ of logs is close throughout (median $+0.026$ for men, $+0.041$ for women).
The post-2006 extrapolated block behaves noticeably better on the tails than the
tight-cap block does, which is a statement about the 2000--04 shapes it freezes, not
independent evidence that the extrapolation is right.
